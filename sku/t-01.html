What it is

Transmission operations, architected end to end.

T-01 is a reference architecture covering the full transmission operations stack: real-time monitoring and control (EMS/SCADA), congestion and study workflows, FERC Order 890 obligations (OASIS posting, ATC calculation, tagging), market interactions with the relevant ISO/RTO, and the OT/IT seam where reliability operations meet enterprise IT.

It is built on the substrate every utility-industry transmission engineer recognizes: control areas, balancing authorities, scheduling entities, NERC functional model boundaries. The architecture doesn't invent a new operating model — it formalizes the one that already exists.

Problem & boundary

What T-01 solves — and where it stops.

What T-01 solves

Transmission operations architecture gets rebuilt every time a utility replaces its EMS or joins/leaves a market. The reference is implicit in the heads of senior architects, not written down. T-01 writes it down.

  • EMS replacement projects that lose months because the integration points weren't modeled in advance.
  • OT/IT seam designs that look fine until CIP-005 audit finds the gap.
  • Market participation projects that learn the settlement and tagging architecture from the ISO docs the hard way.
  • Congestion management workflows where SAMC, study coordinator, and outage scheduler are doing the same job three times.
  • FERC 890 audits that surface OASIS posting drift years after the original integration.

What T-01 deliberately excludes

T-01 is the transmission ops reference architecture. It is not your EMS, not your market participation strategy, not your distribution-side operations.

  • EMS vendor selection or implementation (you do this with your vendor, T-01 informs the requirements).
  • Distribution operations (OMS, ADMS, FLISR) — that's D-03 on the roadmap.
  • Generation dispatch optimization or unit commitment — generation operations stays out of scope.
  • Real-time market trading strategy — T-01 is architecture, not market participation playbook.
  • NERC standards outside CIP, TOP, IRO, MOD (e.g., PRC for protection systems) — scoped to v1.1+.
ISO / RTO market coverage

Which markets v1 covers, at what depth

v1 covers the four ISOs/RTOs where the patterns are most stable and the studio's direct operating experience is deepest. Other markets are addressable in v1.1 — explicitly called out so a buyer in PJM or NYISO knows the gap up front.

Deep coverage in v1

  • CAISO — day-ahead, real-time, FRP, FERC 2222
  • SPP — Integrated Marketplace, FTR auctions
  • MISO — day-ahead, real-time, FTR allocations
  • Bilateral / non-ISO — pattern for non-RTO transmission owners (e.g., NWPP footprint)

v1.1 expansion candidates

  • PJM — capacity market mechanics differ enough to warrant a dedicated pattern
  • ERCOT — different reliability framework (TRE not NERC), separate architecture overlay
  • NYISO — capacity and ancillary services patterns
  • ISO-NE — capacity, ancillary, retirement mechanics

Build status & open questions

What's still being decided

  • ERCOT scope ERCOT operates under TRE, not NERC, with materially different reliability framework. Decision: include as v1.1 separate overlay, or hold for D-04-ish dedicated ERCOT pack?
  • FERC Order 2222 depth DER participation in wholesale markets is rolling out unevenly across ISOs. v1 has the architectural skeleton; the question is whether ISO-by-ISO Order 2222 implementation is part of T-01 or pulled into D-01.
  • Real-time market settlement depth Architecture vs. settlement-detail depth. Architecture-only keeps T-01 focused; settlement detail risks bloating into commercial territory.
  • Vendor substitution notes Currently four vendors documented (OSI, GE, Hitachi-ABB, Siemens). v1.1 candidates: Open Systems International AlertOS, GE PSEC, smaller EMS providers.
  • RC West / Peak RC / regional reliability coordinator interactions Different RCs interact differently with TOPs. Baseline pattern in v1; RC-specific guidance for v1.1.
